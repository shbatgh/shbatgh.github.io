I’m starting at Carnegie Mellon’s School of Computer Science this fall as a sophomore transfer in computational biology, after a year studying computer science and mathematics at the University of Maryland.

Most of my work is in machine learning for biology: single-cell perturbation modeling, microscopy, multimodal representation learning, and the infrastructure that makes large scientific datasets workable. Since November 2025 I’ve worked in Wei Li’s lab at the University of Maryland Institute for Health Computing, continuing full-time through summer 2026. The genomics projects on this site come out of that work — optimal-transport and flow-matching models for control-to-perturbed cell-state translation, LoRA adaptation of single-cell foundation models, and benchmarks for paired MERFISH + multichannel microscopy data.

Before that I built BioVision, a Python/PyTorch pipeline that segments time-lapse microscopy stacks, reconstructs 3D cell meshes, and quantifies cell movement in Drosophila germaria. It was a Regeneron ISEF 2025 finalist and is in use with Columbia’s Kalderon Lab for live-tissue stem-cell analysis.

I also like systems projects where the hard part is turning messy data streams into something precise: a Kalshi/Polymarket arbitrage monitor with semantic market matching and live WebSocket order books, and a small C++ autograd and neural-network framework written from scratch.
